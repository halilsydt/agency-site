## Dev Notes

### Why
The legal pages are (with FAQ) the **simplest** Epic 6 page re-skin: **two structurally-identical, low-interaction, text-only pages** with **no net-new bespoke JS components**. The Atlas structure is two parts: a left-aligned `.phero` hero (green `.mark` on the last title word) → a single-column `.legal` typographic body (760px) with a `.upd` line, a green-soft `.callout` summary, and the `h2`/`p` section flow. **Almost everything mirrors existing work**: the **hero ≈ `AboutHero` (6.8)** (left `.phero` + `heroTitle` triple + glow), the **`.wrap`-over-`Container`** migration is the 6.9 precedent, and the **`<Reveal>`** is the shared 6.5 utility. The only genuinely new surfaces are the `.legal` prose styling (Atlas `h2`/`p`/`.upd`/`.callout` margins) and the `.callout` card — both pure CSS-token work.

**The single biggest risk is content substitution (AC6).** The Atlas legal HTML ships *placeholder* copy (different sections, embedded links/lists) — but the site already has **real, localized legal content** in `content/{en,tr}/legal/*.json`, and four tests (`privacy.test.tsx`, `terms.test.tsx`, `content-privacy.test.ts`, `content-terms.test.ts`) assert on that exact content (section ids, "plausible"/"formspree"/"cal.com", "we take your privacy seriously", "welcome to scalenty", "results", titles). **Re-skin the layout; keep the JSON content verbatim.** A dev who copies Atlas's placeholder sections into the JSON will redden all four tests.
